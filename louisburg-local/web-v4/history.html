The land before the town

Before 1854

A story that starts before Louisburg

The area that became Louisburg sits within a much older Indigenous history. Confederated Peoria peoples — including Peoria, Kaskaskia, Wea and Piankashaw communities — held reservation lands in this part of eastern Kansas before federal land cessions opened much of the area to non-Native settlement.

1854

Treaty, land cession and a new settlement era

On May 30, 1854, the United States signed a treaty with the united Kaskaskia, Peoria, Piankeshaw and Wea tribes. Louisburg's own comprehensive history places the arrival of new farming and home-building in this same period.

John W. Chaudoins and the future townsite

Historical accounts identify John W. Chaudoins as an early non-Native settler at the future Louisburg townsite. The 1883 Cutler history says he purchased land here in 1857, when the area did not yet have a wagon road.

1868–1883

A town takes shape

Founding milestone

November 10, 1868 — Louisburg is laid out.

Charles Sims, Dr. R. F. Steger and D. L. Peery surveyed and laid out the community. The City still recognizes November 10 as Louisburg's founding day. Early sources called the settlement New St. Louis or Little St. Louis before the Louisburg name took hold.

1883 Cutler history ↗Early written account
1868

A Broadway building survives from the beginning

The Historic Preservation Commission identifies 120 S. Broadway as standing since 1868, making it one of the oldest continuously used commercial buildings on Broadway. It was restored again in 2022.

Confirmed

c. 1870–72

Little St. Louis becomes Louisburg

The sources agree on the story but not the exact year. The railroad-era name caused confusion with St. Louis, Missouri. Historical sources place the change around 1870, 1871 or by 1872, so Louisburg Local preserves the range instead of inventing certainty.

c. 1875

The railroad splits the settlement — and downtown shifts south

Louisburg's comprehensive plan describes the railroad dividing the early community. Around 1875, homes and businesses increasingly moved south of the tracks toward the Broadway downtown known today. Mills and fruit-growing businesses were already part of the local economy.

Confirmed

Louisburg gets a newspaper voice

The Louisville and Miami Herald was established July 4, 1876. By the early 1880s, the newspaper was part of a surprisingly active commercial town.

Louisburg incorporated as a third-class city in 1882. The first city election followed in November, and Samuel W. Moore became mayor.

1895–1952

Fire, cars, schools and a changing Broadway

1895

The first organized fire-protection effort

On March 15, 1895, Jesse Williams was asked to build a hook-and-ladder fire apparatus for the city — the opening chapter of the Louisburg Fire Department's recorded history.

Confirmed
Louisburg Fire Department history ↗City + Historical Society research
1909

Fire destroys City Hall and a Broadway block

On November 18, 1909, fire destroyed City Hall, burned city records and wiped out the west-side Broadway block between Amity and South First. Reported damage was about $3,000.

Confirmed
Fire Department history ↗Louisburg Herald research
1917–19

A fire truck — then an organized crew

The City purchased a fire truck in 1917. In December 1919, Mayor Tronjo appointed R. Henry Reed fire chief and named an organized engine crew.

Confirmed
Fire Department history ↗City history

Louisburg's first dedicated fire station

The community's first fire station opened in the former city power plant near Broadway and South Second Street.

Confirmed

1925

Downtown burns again

Several businesses on the east side of Broadway were destroyed by fire. Reported damage reached about $50,000 — a major blow to the commercial core.

Confirmed

1926

Louisburg Rural High School opens

The high school opened one block east of Broadway in 1926. Generations of students attended there until 1992, making the building one of the strongest shared-memory landmarks in town.

Confirmed

1930s

The Little Round House and the highway era

As truck traffic increased on K-68, the distinctive Little Round House served as a Kansas port-of-entry/inspection station. Today it survives at City Lake as a small piece of transportation history.

Confirmed

1947

The Skelly station photograph captures a changing town

A memorable 1947 photograph shows the unusual Amity-and-Broadway building operating as a Skelly station, with an attendant pumping air into students' bicycle tires — a perfect visual of Louisburg in the automobile age.

A. D. Fox donated the building that became Fox Hall to Louisburg in 1952, directing that it remain a place for meetings, dances, parties, plays and community gatherings.

The city celebrated its centennial and buried a time capsule. Fifty years later, that capsule would become a centerpiece of the 2018 sesquicentennial celebration.

A roadside cider stand begins

On an 80-acre farm outside Louisburg, the Louisburg Cider Mill began pressing and selling cider commercially in 1977. What started in a small barn grew into one of the community's most recognized regional attractions.

2000–NOW

A small town grows fast — and keeps its memory

2000–10

Louisburg's population surges

The city grew from about 2,687 residents in 2000 to 4,315 in 2010 — roughly a 61% increase in one decade. It is one of the clearest turning points in modern Louisburg.

Confirmed
Comprehensive Plan ↗Population history
2009

City Lake park is dedicated to Ron Weers

In June 2009 the Governing Body dedicated the original City Lake park in memory of Ron Weers, a longtime Louisburg builder and community supporter who helped with local fields and civic construction projects.

2014–15

Downtown planning and a new City Hall

Louisburg adopted a Downtown Revitalization Plan in 2014. In the following years, the historic Broadway core continued to be reinvested in, including the adaptive reuse of an older commercial building as City Hall.

150 years — and a message to 2068

Louisburg celebrated its sesquicentennial throughout 2018, with the final celebration on founding day, November 10. The 1968 time capsule was opened and a new capsule was prepared for 2068.
